# 演讲者 A：开场与第 1–4 段

时间：0:00–13:00，约 13 分钟。操作电脑的人：B。总览见 [0 总览与时间表](0%20总览与时间表.md)。

## 注意事项

- **你的任务是把问题立起来。** 你这一段不需要给出作者的答案，只要让同学清楚两件事：量化幸福背后有两个预设；对快乐有两种不同的看法。
- **开场讨论控制在 3 分半以内。** 举手投票加两位同学各说一句理由就够了。同学说得再好也不要追问第三个人。
- **举手投票后点人数最多的那张卡片**，让屏幕上有个选中的状态，然后再请人说理由。
- **“查看标准答案”是可删内容。** 它是一个小玩笑（用道法答题模板回答人生问题），能活跃气氛，但要花 40 秒。时间紧就不点。
- **第 2 段的两个预设要让同学自己找。** 你问 “What are the two assumptions?”，等 5 秒，请一位同学读出来，再点 “Reveal this stage”。
- **第 3、4 段要放在一起比较。** 讲完第 3 段不要停，直接说 “By contrast” 进入第 4 段。这两段的重点只有一个：快乐是目标，还是副产品。
- **第 4 段讲完不要点 “Reveal this stage”。** 阶段二包括第 3–6 段，由 B 在第 6 段后揭示。
- **发音：** quantify /ˈkwɑːntɪfaɪ/；hedonic /hiːˈdɑːnɪk/（重音在第二个音节，可以点屏幕上的 US 按钮让大家听）；virtue /ˈvɜːrtʃuː/；virtuous /ˈvɜːrtʃuəs/；contentment 重音在 -tent-；detract 重音在 -tract。
- **语速：** 念原文句子时放慢，念完停一秒再解释。
- **超时就删：** 先删“标准答案”，再删第 3 段里关于 *sets a seal on* 的那两句。

## 演讲稿

### 一、开场与读前讨论（0:00–3:30）

`[屏幕：引子页]`

Good morning, everyone. We are Group __. Today we are going to read one text together. It is called *Happiness, quantified*, and it comes from the magazine *New Philosopher*.

I'm __, and I will take you through the first four paragraphs. After that, __ and __ will take over.

Before we read, I'd like to ask you a question. Please look at the screen. Here are three kinds of life.

Life A is a pleasant, comfortable life with few worries.

Life B has clear goals and meaningful work, but it often comes with stress and anxiety.

Life C has joy, but also sadness and conflict, and you are willing to live through them all.

Which life would you choose, and why? Take ten seconds to decide.

`[停 10 秒]`

Hands up if you chose A. Thank you. B? And C?

`[B 点击人数最多的卡片]`

Could someone who chose __ tell us why? One sentence is enough.

`[请一位同学]`

Thank you. Now, someone who made a different choice?

`[请第二位同学]`

Thank you. There is no right answer here, and in real life these three may overlap. But please remember your choice, because the author of this text also makes a choice, and it may not be the one you expect.

`[可删：B 点“查看标准答案”]` If this were a politics exam, you would probably write something like this: four points, each one neatly worded, full marks. But look at the question at the bottom. Must sadness help us grow to deserve a place in our lives? Our text will give a different kind of answer. `[B 收起答案]`

So here is the one question for today's class: **more happiness, or a fuller experience of life?** We won't answer it now. Let's read.

`[B 点“进入带读”]`

### 二、第 1–2 段：量化幸福和它的预设（3:30–8:00）

`[屏幕：第 1 段]`

Paragraph one begins with a very simple question: *How happy are you?*

It sounds like a question nobody can answer exactly. But the author tells us that in positive psychology, there are plenty of tests and techniques to help you answer it.

Let's look at three words. `[B 依次点 realm、quantify、contentment]`

*Realm* means an area or field. "In the realm of positive psychology" means in the field of positive psychology.

*Quantify* is the key word of the whole text. It means to express something as a number. Its noun is *quantification*, and you can already see it in the title: *Happiness, quantified*.

*Contentment* is a feeling of being satisfied with your life. It is quieter than excitement and it lasts longer.

So paragraph one describes a phenomenon. People today measure their happiness on scales, or they download apps to do it for them. Has anyone here ever taken a test like this, or used an app that tracks your mood?

`[等 3 秒，有人回应就点头，没有就继续]`

Here is my question for paragraph two. `[指屏幕上的问题]` Behind these tests and apps, what is being assumed?

`[B 点“下一段”]`

Please read paragraph two silently and find the two assumptions.

`[停 15 秒]`

Who can read them out for us?

`[请一位同学]`

Exactly. First, happiness can be quantified. Second, your mission in life is to maximize this quantity, and to minimize whatever detracts from it.

Two expressions here. `[B 点 underlie、detracts from]` *Underlie* means to be the hidden basis of something. Assumptions are not on the surface. You have to dig for them. And *detract from* means to make something less good. So "whatever detracts from it" means anything that reduces your happiness, such as sadness, anger or pain.

Now think about the second assumption for a moment. Even if we *can* measure happiness, does it follow that we *should* maximize it? The author doesn't answer yet. But this is the idea the whole text is going to question.

`[B 点“Reveal this stage”]`

So this is our first stage. The author starts from a phenomenon and digs out the assumptions under it.

### 三、第 3–4 段：两种幸福观（8:00–13:00）

If maximizing happiness is your goal in life, what view of life is behind it? Paragraph three gives it a name.

`[B 点“下一段”，到第 3 段]`

The name is in the first sentence: *a hedonic view of the world*. `[B 点 hedonic 的 US 发音]` Hedonic. It describes a view in which pleasure is the most important thing in life. In the author's words, "pleasure is both the key and the surest guide to living well."

Please notice the next sentence, because it prevents a misunderstanding. "This doesn't mean spending all your time partying, but it does mean being unafraid to make pleasure-seeking your primary aim." So a hedonic view is not about being lazy or wild. It simply puts pleasure first.

`[可删]` One more phrase: *sets a seal on*. `[B 点 sets a seal on]` It means to confirm something. In this view, even morality, the question of right and wrong, becomes a matter of maximizing happiness. Quantification confirms that idea. `[可删结束]`

But this is not the only way to think about happiness. Look at the first two words of paragraph four.

`[B 点“下一段”，到第 4 段]`

*By contrast.* These two words tell us that a different view is coming.

This second view connects happiness with *virtue* and *purpose*. `[B 点 virtue、as an end in itself]` Virtue means moral goodness, and purpose here means your aim in life. People who hold this view do not treat pleasure "as an end in itself", that is, as the final goal. Instead, they regard it as "a by-product of doing the right thing."

A by-product is something you get while you are making something else. So in this view, you don't chase pleasure. You do the right thing, and pleasure follows.

The last sentence adds one more point. A virtuous person can tell two kinds of pleasure apart. `[B 点 base、elevated]` On one side, there are "base, self-indulgent satisfactions", which are low and selfish pleasures. On the other side, there are "the elevated delights of excellence", the higher joy of doing something really well. For example, compare the pleasure of scrolling on your phone for three hours with the pleasure of finally solving a hard maths problem.

So let me put paragraphs three and four side by side. Both are about pleasure. In the hedonic view, pleasure is the primary aim. In the virtue view, pleasure is a by-product. The question between them is: what place does pleasure have in a good life?

These two views sound like opposites. But in real life, do people really have to choose between pleasure and purpose? And if they disagree about pleasure, do they also disagree about measuring happiness? __ will take you through the next part.

`[交给 B]`
