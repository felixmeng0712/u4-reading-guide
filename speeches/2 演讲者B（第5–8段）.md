## 演讲稿

### 一、第 5–6 段：差异之下的共同点（13:00–18:00）

`[屏幕：第 4 段。C 点“下一段”，到第 5 段]`

Thank you, __. So we have two views that sound like opposites. In one, pleasure is the aim. In the other, pleasure is a by-product. Do we have to choose?

Paragraph five says no. Look at the first two words: *In practice*. In theory, the two views are opposed. In practice, "most people accept that both pleasure and purpose play a part in happiness."

So how do you measure a happiness that includes both? The text gives one modern answer: *subjective well-being*. `[C 点 account for]` *Account for* means to take something into consideration. This measure tries to take both pleasure and purpose into consideration.

And how does it work? It asks people how they experience their own lives, "on a scale from 1 to 10."

`[可删]` Let's try it. On a scale from 1 to 10, how satisfied are you with your sleep this week? Just show me with your fingers. `[看一眼，笑一下]` I see. That is subjective well-being, and you have just been quantified. `[可删结束]`

Notice what has happened here. We started with two different views of happiness, but we are back to measuring again.

`[C 点“下一段”，到第 6 段]`

Paragraph six goes one step further, and here is the most important word: *surprisingly*. "The spirit of quantification is surprisingly compatible with Aristotle's version of virtue ethics."

`[C 点 compatible]` *Compatible* means able to exist together. Why is this surprising? Because, as __ showed us, virtue ethics does not treat pleasure as the goal. So we might expect it to reject the whole idea of maximizing.

But the author points out two things. The ancient Greeks were *prescriptive* about what virtue was. `[C 点 prescriptive]` That is, they gave clear rules. And they demanded *emotional self-mastery*, the control of your own emotions. The paragraph ends: "Emotional well-being was a known quantity to be maximized."

So even this older view treats your emotional life as something to manage and improve.

`[C 点“Reveal this stage”]`

Here is our second stage. Paragraphs three and four compare two views. Paragraphs five and six then show what they share. They disagree about pleasure, but both are compatible with quantification. Both treat our inner life as something to be optimized.

### 二、第 7 段：作者的转向（18:00–22:00）

So far, every view we have read is about how to evaluate life, or how to optimize it. Here is my question: does the author accept this starting point?

`[C 点“下一段”，到第 7 段]`

Please read paragraph seven silently. It contains two questions. Find them, and decide which one the author chooses.

`[停 20 秒]`

The first question: "Are emotions an index of life's successes and failures, to be rationally optimized and wrestled into harmony?"

`[C 点 index、wrestled into harmony]` An *index* is a sign or a measure, like a score that tells you how well your life is going. And *wrestled into harmony* is a vivid picture. To wrestle is to fight with your whole body. So in this first view, you fight your emotions until they are in order.

The second question: "Or are they something else: neither measures nor information, but the very stuff of life itself?"

*The very stuff of life itself.* In this view, emotions are not a report about your life. They *are* your life.

Which one does the author choose? Who can find the words?

`[请一位同学]`

Yes: "I find myself increasingly drawn to this latter view." `[C 点 drawn to]` *This latter view* points to the second question. And the author adds that they are "increasingly uninterested in cultivating an optimal inner life." *Optimal* means the best possible.

This is the turning point of the whole text. Until now, the question was how to get more happiness. From here on, the author is no longer asking that question at all.

`[C 点“Reveal this stage”]`

Stage three is only one paragraph, but everything turns on it: from optimizing to experiencing.

### 三、第 8 段：悲伤和愤怒的位置（22:00–27:00）

If emotions are the stuff of life itself, how does that change our attitude to sadness and anger? Paragraph eight answers with the author's own family.

`[C 点“下一段”，到第 8 段]`

It begins with a reason: "This is perhaps because I no longer believe there's any hierarchy or a dichotomy between thoughts and feelings."

`[C 点 hierarchy 和 dichotomy 的 US 发音]` A *hierarchy* is a system with higher and lower levels. A *dichotomy* is a division into two opposite things. So the author no longer believes that thoughts are higher than feelings, or that the two are opposites.

Then comes the family. At work, the author deals in words and reasons. "But at home, emotion reigns." `[C 点 reigns、ripple]` *Reign* is what a king or queen does. At home, emotion is in charge. And love, sadness, anger and joy "ripple through our household", like small waves spreading across water, "pushing us apart and bringing us together."

Now the two sentences I most want you to remember. Let's read them together.

"They are not enemies to overcome or data to be processed, but part of who we are."

"The trick is not to will or wish them away, but to notice them."

`[带全班齐读，每句一遍]`

Look back at paragraph two. There, sadness and anger were things that "detract from" happiness, things to minimize. Here, they are "part of who we are." That is how far the text has moved.

Before I hand over, one more thing. Our task asks us to analyse three long sentences. Two of them are in the original text, and __ will show you those. The third one is here.

`[C 点“AI 改写练习 · 基于本段”，再点“展开结构解析”]`

Please note the label: this sentence is **not** from the original text. We used AI to rewrite the idea of paragraph eight as one long sentence, for practice.

Don't try to read it all at once. Find the skeleton first. It is highlighted: "Were we to treat sadness and anger as enemies, we would risk overlooking the possibility…"

Two grammar points. First, "Were we to treat" equals "If we were to treat". The *if* is dropped and *were* moves to the front. It describes something unreal. Second, look at "it is precisely those feelings… that reveal what matters to us." This is an emphatic sentence, *it is… that…*, and it stresses "those feelings".

The meaning is the same as the original: the feelings we most want to push away are often the ones that show what matters to us.

So sadness and anger have a place in a family. But what does this mean for the way each of us lives every day? __ will take us to the end of the text.
