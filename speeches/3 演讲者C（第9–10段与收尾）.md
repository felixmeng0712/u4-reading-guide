## 演讲稿

### 一、第 9 段：充分地活着（27:00–32:00）

`[屏幕：第 8 段。A 点“词与词组”回到词汇栏，再点“下一段”，到第 9 段]`

Thank you, __. Paragraph eight was about one family. Paragraph nine turns that into an attitude to life for all of us, and it gives the attitude a name. Look at the first three words: *Being fully alive*.

The author first tells us what it is not. "Being fully alive isn't just about existing from moment to moment." Existing is not the same as living.

Then, what it is: "It means attending to and accepting whatever you're experiencing: in your inner life, in the world around you."

`[A 点 attending to]` There are two verbs here. *Attend to* means to pay attention to something. And *accept* means you do not fight it. Paying attention comes first, then accepting. There are also two places: your inner life, which means your feelings, and the world around you.

Now look at the list in the next sentence. "You may be full of exaltation, grief, excitement, pain, rage, confusion, fear, hope." `[A 点 exaltation、grief、rage]` *Exaltation* is very great joy. *Grief* is deep sadness, often after a loss. *Rage* is violent anger.

How many of these eight are pleasant feelings? `[等 3 秒]` Only three: exaltation, excitement and hope. The rest are painful. And the author puts them all in one list, without ranking them. That is what "no hierarchy" looks like in a sentence.

Then a very short sentence: "You are alive until you are not." Seven words. After the long list, it makes us stop.

And now the hardest sentence in the text. `[A 点“长难句 ↗”，再点“展开结构解析”]`

"And at every single moment of that experience is your life, waiting for you to make of it what you will."

Where is the subject? `[等 3 秒]` It is not at the beginning. The subject is *your life*, and it comes after *is*. This is inversion. In normal order, it would be: "Your life is at every single moment of that experience." The author moves "at every single moment" to the front, so that we hear the moments first.

And the ending: *make of it what you will*. "It" is your life. The phrase means: give it the meaning you choose, and shape it as you wish.

So your life is not a score that you check at the end of the year. It is there in every single moment, including the sad and angry ones, and it is waiting for you.

### 二、第 10 段：另一种“最大化”（32:00–35:30）

Now, a question before the last paragraph. In paragraph two, the author questioned the idea of maximizing happiness. So why does the word *maximization* come back at the very end?

`[A 点“词与词组”，再点“下一段”，到第 10 段]`

Paragraph ten is only two sentences. Let's read it together.

`[带全班齐读]`

"This is a very different kind of quantification. There is, it suggests, one kind of maximization that matters more than any other: how fully you are able to inhabit your own experiences."

`[A 点“长难句 ↗”]` The structure is simpler than it looks. Take out "it suggests". It is only inserted. What is left is: "There is one kind of maximization that matters more than any other." And after the colon, the author tells us what that maximization is: "how fully you are able to inhabit your own experiences."

`[A 点“词与词组”，再点 inhabit]` *Inhabit* normally means to live in a place. Here it is a metaphor. You live inside your own experiences, instead of standing outside them and giving them a score.

So let's compare. In paragraph two, what is being maximized? `[等同学回答：happiness / the quantity of happiness]` Yes, the quantity of happiness. And in paragraph ten? `[等同学回答]` How fully you live your experiences, all of them.

The word is the same, but its object has changed. The author borrows the language of quantification in order to say something that cannot be put on a scale from 1 to 10. The ending also answers the title. Happiness, quantified? Yes, but in "a very different kind of quantification."

`[A 点“Reveal this stage”]`

That is stage four: from one family's experience, to an attitude to life, and back to the opening.

### 三、完整结构与开场问题（35:30–39:00）

Now let's put the whole text on one page.

`[A 点“View full text structure”，页面滚到结构图]`

At the top is the question we started with: more happiness, or a fuller experience of life?

`[从左到右指着讲]`

Paragraphs one and two introduce the topic. Happiness can be measured, and behind that are two assumptions.

Paragraphs three to six compare views. The hedonic view and the virtue view disagree about pleasure, but both are compatible with quantification.

Paragraph seven is the turn. Are emotions an index of life, or the very stuff of life itself? The author is drawn to the second.

Paragraphs eight to ten conclude. Sadness and anger are part of who we are. Being fully alive means attending to and accepting whatever you are experiencing.

So the text moves in four steps: introduce, compare, turn, conclude. And at the bottom is the author's answer. What matters most is not how much happiness you can measure, but how fully you are able to inhabit your own experiences.

`[A 向上滚回右栏，指“Back to the opening question”]`

Let me ask you to do it yourselves. Can someone sum up the author's answer in one sentence, and give us one line from the text to support it?

`[请一位同学；说完后 A 点“See a sample answer”]`

Thank you. Here is one possible answer, and the line we chose is from paragraph eight: "They are not enemies to overcome or data to be processed, but part of who we are."

One thing to be careful about. The author does **not** prove that happiness cannot be measured, and the ending does not offer a new scale. What the author questions is making the maximization of happiness the goal of life.

At the beginning, __ asked you to choose between three lives. Would anyone change their choice now? `[停 3 秒，看一圈]` You don't have to tell us. Whichever you chose, the author would probably say this: if you are living life C, with its joy and its sadness, you are not failing at happiness. You are fully alive.
